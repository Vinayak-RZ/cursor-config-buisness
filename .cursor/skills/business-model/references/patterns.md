# Business model patterns (St. Gallen, for agents)

Source: Gassmann, Frankenberger, Csik — St. Gallen Business Model Navigator (working paper + *The Business Model Navigator*). ~90% of "new" models recombine existing patterns. Use **pattern confrontation**: take a card that feels unrelated and ask "if this were our model, what would change?"

This is a working catalog, not a license to copy the book. Keep descriptions short; look up a pattern on [businessmodelnavigator.com/explore](https://businessmodelnavigator.com/explore) if you need the full card.

---

## How to use

1. Name the **dominant industry logic** (what everyone copies).
2. Pick 2–4 patterns — at least one "nearby" and one "distant."
3. For each, rewrite Who / What / How / Value.
4. Drop patterns that break a hard constraint (regulation, liquidity, capital).
5. Combine only if the economics still close (e.g. Freemium + Subscription, Razor-and-blade + Lock-in).

---

## Patterns that often work (test anyway)

These show up repeatedly in software, marketplaces, and productized services. "Work" means a known economic engine — not "easy."

| Pattern | Works when | Fails when |
|---------|------------|------------|
| **Subscription** | Ongoing job, habit, or environment; switching cost or habit retains | One-time job; customers refuse recurring; churn > value created |
| **Freemium** | Very cheap marginal cost; viral/network; a clear paid step-up | High cost-to-serve free users; no wedge to paid; "free" trains no-pay |
| **Pay per use** | Value tracks consumption (API, energy, transactions) | Buyer needs budget certainty; usage anxiety; you cannot meter cheaply |
| **Flat rate** | Usage variance is your friend; simplicity sells | Heavy users destroy margin; light users feel cheated and churn |
| **Two-sided market** | Cross-side network effects; you can subsidize one side | Chicken-and-egg you cannot seed; take-rate without liquidity |
| **Hidden revenue** (ads / data / third-party pay) | Attention or data is the product; user ≠ payer | Low volume; brand-sensitive users; privacy/regulatory wall |
| **Razor and blade** | Consumable or complement with lock-in | Customers buy generic blades; hardware subsidy never recouped |
| **Lock-in** | Workflow embedding, data gravity, tech complementarity | Lock-in without value = resentment + regulation; easy export |
| **Solution provider** | Customer will pay to collapse vendors into one throat to choke | You cannot operationally own the full job; services don't scale |
| **Layer player** | One step of many value chains; scale/specialization | The layer gets commoditized; platforms disintermediate you |
| **Orchestrator** | You win by coordinating, not owning the chain | Coordination costs > savings; quality/reputation leaks |
| **Direct selling** + **Digitization** | Margin in skipping channel; product can be delivered digitally | Channel partners *are* the distribution; you cannot replace trust |
| **Licensing** | IP is the scarce asset; others distribute better | Weak IP; licensees become competitors |
| **Add-on** | Base offer is a door-opener; extras have high willingness to pay | Base is a trap; sticker shock; procurement blocks extras |
| **Performance-based contracting** | Outcome is measurable; you control enough of the result | Outcome not measurable; customer causes the miss |
| **Make more of it** | Slack capability (AWS-from-retail) has outside demand | Distracts from core; capability isn't actually distinctive |
| **Long tail** | Near-zero marginal distribution; discovery works | Inventory/support costs scale with SKUs |
| **No frills** | A segment will trade service for price | Race to bottom; you cut the thing customers actually hired |
| **Open source** + services | Adoption is the funnel; support/hosting captures | You cannot capture; cloud hosts eat the surplus |
| **Peer-to-peer** | Homogeneous spare capacity; trust layer is the product | Trust/safety costs; regulation; liquidity |

**SaaS default (still a hypothesis):** Subscription or hybrid Subscription + Pay-per-use, sometimes Freemium as distribution. Validate willingness to pay before building the billing cathedral.

**Marketplace default:** Two-sided market + take-rate (Affiliation / Revenue sharing). Solve liquidity first; take-rate later.

**Hardware + consumable:** Razor and blade + Lock-in. Watch generic refill.

**Attention products:** Hidden revenue. Only if you can get to scale without destroying the user job.

## Full pattern index (ideation)

Use as confrontation cards. One-line logic; numbers match common Navigator lists.

1. **Add-on** — cheap core, extras make the real price  
2. **Affiliation** — pay others to originate the sale  
3. **Aikido** — value opposite the mainstream  
4. **Auction** — price discovery via bids  
5. **Barter** — exchange without (full) cash  
6. **Cash machine** — customer pays before you fund COGS (liquidity)  
7. **Cross-selling** — extra category on existing access  
8. **Crowdfunding** — demand and capital from a crowd  
9. **Crowdsourcing** — crowd produces the solution  
10. **Customer loyalty** — rewards / identity bind future revenue  
11. **Digitization** — same job, digital delivery  
12. **Direct selling** — skip the intermediary  
13. **E-commerce** — sell through online channel only  
14. **Experience selling** — charge for the experience wrapper  
15. **Flat rate** — one fee, unlimited (or uncapped-feeling) use  
16. **Fractional ownership** — share a capital asset  
17. **Franchising** — license brand + system; franchisee operates  
18. **Freemium** — free tier finances paid tier via mix  
19. **From push to pull** — decentralize to follow demand  
20. **Guaranteed availability** — sell uptime / no downtime  
21. **Hidden revenue** — third party pays, user may not  
22. **Ingredient branding** — branded component inside the product  
23. **Integrator** — own most of the value chain  
24. **Layer player** — one step, many chains  
25. **Leverage customer data** — data as product or lever (mind law)  
26. **License** — sell rights to IP, not the ops  
27. **Lock-in** — switching costs protect the base  
28. **Long tail** — many niches beat a few hits  
29. **Make more of it** — sell slack assets/know-how outward  
30. **Mass customization** — modular variety at near-mass cost  
31. **No frills** — strip to the job, pass savings  
32. **Open business** — ecosystem co-creates  
33. **Open source** — free core, paid complements  
34. **Orchestrator** — coordinate specialists

37. **Peer-to-peer** — match equals with spare capacity

40. **Rent instead of buy** — access > ownership  
41. **Revenue sharing** — split with complementors  
42. **Reverse engineering** — compatible/cheaper follow (legal risk)  
43. **Reverse innovation** — emerging-market design sold upmarket  
44. **Robin Hood** — premium funds access for the poor

56. **Sensor as a service** — hardware enables data/service revenue  
57. **Virtualization** — physical process in software  
58. **Object self-service** — thing reorders itself  
59. **Object as point of sale** — consumable sold at consumption  
60. **Prosumer** — customer is also producer

## Combination examples (recombination, not originality)

- Salesforce-class software: **Subscription** + **Solution provider** (implementation) + sometimes **Add-on**  
- Spotify-class: **Freemium** + **Subscription** + **Hidden revenue** (ads on free)  
- Gillette / Nespresso / printers: **Razor and blade** + **Lock-in**  
- AWS: **Pay per use** + **Make more of it** (retail spare capacity)  
- Apple App Store-class: **Two-sided market** + **Revenue sharing** + **Lock-in**  
- Open-core infra: **Open source** + **Subscription** (hosted / support)  
- Marketplaces: **Two-sided market** + **Affiliation** / take-rate; sometimes **Guaranteed availability** as trust

When combining, re-run the economics gate. Two patterns that each "work" can break together (e.g. Freemium + high-touch Solution provider).
