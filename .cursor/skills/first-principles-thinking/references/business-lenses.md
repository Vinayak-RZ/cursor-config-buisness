# Business lenses — first-principles decompositions

Use when the reconstruction target is a startup, pricing, GTM, product, or operations question. Do not start from clichés (raise, PLG, add AI, copy competitor pricing, launch on every channel) unless the decomposition supports them.

---

## Is this a good idea?

Reframe:

- Is the problem painful enough, frequent enough, and urgent enough?
- Who feels it most acutely?
- What do they do today, and why is that insufficient?
- Can value be delivered at attractive unit economics?

## How do we compete?

True bases of competition (pick the real one): cost, speed, trust, workflow fit, integration, brand, switching cost, data, distribution. Which of these are hard reality vs inherited market structure?

## What is the moat?

A moat is a mechanism, not a slogan. Sources: proprietary data, workflow embedding, switching costs, cost advantage, trust, speed, unique distribution, network effects, ecosystem position, scarce supply. Do not claim moat from "AI" by default.

## Pricing

Break into: value created, value captured, buyer budget, alternatives, purchase authority, measurable ROI, risk reduction, speed/convenience.

Ask: is the buyer paying for seats, usage, outcome, convenience, money saved, or risk reduced? Which variable matches how value is *felt*?

Fundamentals: perceived value > price > cost to serve (including CAC and support) or the model dies.

## Growth

Break into: attention source → message relevance → trust → conversion friction → onboarding friction → time-to-value → retention loop → referral/expansion.

Do not accept "we need more traffic" until the bottleneck is named.

## Operations / cost

Decompose the expensive line into constituents (people, tools, waste, waiting, rework, compliance). Ask which constituents are physics vs habit. Rebuild the process after deleting steps, not by automating the current mess.
